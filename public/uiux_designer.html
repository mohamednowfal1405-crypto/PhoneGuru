01 · About the role

Design experiences that make phone buying effortless

At PhoneGuru, design is not an afterthought — it is the product. As a UI/UX Designer, you'll be responsible for crafting the visual language and interaction design of everything users see and touch: our phone comparison tool, spec detail pages, AI chatbot interface, price tracker, and the overall site experience across desktop and mobile.

We're looking for someone with 1–4 years of experience who can go from user research and wireframes all the way to polished, developer-ready Figma files. You'll work directly with product managers, frontend engineers, and the founding team — your designs will ship fast and reach millions of users across India every single month.

02 · What you'll do

Responsibilities

  • Own the end-to-end design process for new features — from user research and problem framing, through wireframes and prototypes, to final high-fidelity Figma deliverables.
  • Redesign and continuously improve existing pages including phone spec pages, the comparison tool, search results, and the homepage.
  • Design the UI of PhoneGuru's AI chatbot — conversation flows, response cards, suggestion chips, and error states.
  • Build and maintain PhoneGuru's design system in Figma — components, tokens, spacing rules, typography scale, and dark/light mode variants.
  • Conduct user interviews, usability tests, and heuristic evaluations to surface pain points and validate design decisions with real data.
  • Collaborate closely with frontend developers during handoff — annotating specs, answering implementation questions, and QA-ing shipped work against your designs.
  • Create mobile-first designs and ensure every experience works seamlessly across all screen sizes and devices.
03 · Requirements

What we're looking for

  • Minimum 1 year of professional UI/UX design experience — internships with real shipped work are welcome.
  • Strong proficiency in Figma — components, auto layout, variants, prototyping, and developer handoff.
  • A portfolio showcasing at least 2–3 end-to-end design projects with clear problem statements, process documentation, and final outcomes.
  • Solid understanding of UX fundamentals — information architecture, user flows, wireframing, and usability principles.
  • Good eye for visual design — typography, spacing, colour, and layout that feel intentional and polished.
  • Ability to communicate design decisions clearly to both technical and non-technical stakeholders.
  • Comfortable working in fast-moving environments where designs evolve quickly based on feedback and constraints.
04 · Nice to have

Bonus points

  • Prior experience designing for a tech media, e-commerce, or product comparison platform.
  • Familiarity with basic HTML and CSS — designers who understand how their work gets built make better design decisions.
  • Experience designing AI or conversational UI — chatbots, smart suggestions, or search-as-you-type flows.
  • Motion design or micro-interaction skills using Figma Smart Animate, Lottie, or After Effects.
  • Experience running A/B tests and using analytics tools (Mixpanel, Hotjar, GA4) to inform design choices.
  • A personal design blog, Dribbble profile, or Behance portfolio — we love seeing designers who share their process.

06 · Life at PhoneGuru

What we offer

  • Salary between ₹8 LPA and ₹16 LPA depending on your experience and portfolio quality, reviewed every 12 months.
  • Hybrid work schedule — 3 days in our Bengaluru office (where the whiteboards and coffee are), 2 days from home.
  • ₹25,000 annual learning budget — Figma Advanced courses, design conferences, UX books, whatever sharpens your craft.
  • MacBook of your choice and a ₹10,000 home setup allowance for a monitor, desk accessories, or whatever your workspace needs.
  • Comprehensive health insurance covering you and your immediate family from day one.
  • A seat at the table from the very start — your design decisions directly shape what millions of users experience.
  • Free access to every phone we review — great for hands-on UX research, and yes, sometimes you get to keep one.
